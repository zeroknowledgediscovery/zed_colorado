\origsection{Empirical correspondence in the Colorado ILD analysis}

\subsection{Cohort, provenance, and analysis hierarchy}
The current analysis uses the Colorado genomic--clinical cohort assembled in \texttt{zebra\_comp}. The three literal stored endpoint fields are \texttt{FILD or FILA ADJUDICATED}, \texttt{NONFIBROTIC ILD ADJUDICATED}, and \texttt{NON FIBROTIC ILA ADJUDICATED}; the differing spacing is retained here because it is part of the source-field naming, while later prose and tables use standardized display labels. The historical notebook construction maps \texttt{Y/y} to 1 and \texttt{N/n} to 0, converts the field to numeric, assigns missing target values to 0, and excludes observations without an available 104-week ZeBRA score. Consequently, the target-zero group is an operational comparison group rather than a fully adjudicated control cohort. This distinction is especially important for false-positive-rate interpretation.

The common notebook-32 analysis cohort contains 12,825 subjects. The operational FILD/FILA endpoint has 254 positives, whereas the nonfibrotic ILA and nonfibrotic ILD endpoints have only 74 and 27 positives, respectively. We therefore treat FILD/FILA as the primary empirical endpoint and the two nonfibrotic outcomes as sparse ancillary checks rather than independent replications.

The empirical analyses are hierarchical. Notebook~32 evaluates global discrimination, calibration, stringent operating points, and feature-use stability for ZeBRA, genomic-only, and nonlinear combined models. Notebook~33 tests paired incremental genomic value conditional on ZeBRA in a regularized framework. Separate analyses then test whether ZeBRA reconstructs \textit{MUC5B}, whether conditional \textit{MUC5B} disease information varies across ZeBRA score space, whether a two-threshold genomic rescue can improve held-out decisions, and how ZeBRA and the hybrid occupy the low-FPR ROC geometry.

All figures in this manuscript are generated by PGFPlots at compile time from the canonical CSV outputs under \texttt{../zebra\_comp/RESULTS}; no pre-rendered raster result figure is included. Thus changing the analysis outputs and recompiling the manuscript updates the plotted values directly.

\subsection{Global repeated-split discrimination}
Across 30 repeated held-out outer splits, ZeBRA is the strongest predictor for all three operational endpoints. For FILD/FILA, mean AUC is 0.8142 for ZeBRA, 0.5662 for the selected genomic panel, and 0.7988 for the nonlinear combined model. In the paired notebook-32 comparison, combined-minus-ZeBRA FILD/FILA AUC is negative in all 30 splits (mean $-0.01535$). The sparse nonfibrotic endpoints show the same ordering but are not treated as replication.

The combined learner was not simply blind to the major genomic signal: in the notebook-32 SHAP stability output, the ZeBRA score is a top-20 combined-model feature in 30/30 splits, while the rs35705950 GG indicator is top-20 in 28/30 combined-model splits and 27/30 genomic-only splits. Thus the lack of global improvement should not be interpreted as evidence that the fitted genomic models never used \textit{MUC5B}.

\subsection{Paired incremental value conditional on ZeBRA}
Notebook~33 evaluates ZeBRA-only and ZeBRA-plus-genomics models on the same held-out subjects in 30 repeated splits. For FILD/FILA, the mean paired AUC difference is only $+0.00043$, the median is 0, and the split-wise 2.5th--97.5th percentile range is $[-0.00507,0.00408]$. Average precision decreases, while Brier score and log loss worsen. These are empirical split quantiles, not confidence limits from independent samples.

\subsection{ZeBRA does not reconstruct \textit{MUC5B}}
Among 12,766 subjects with called rs35705950 genotype, ZeBRA predicts T-carrier status at essentially chance level: pooled AUC 0.5088, explicitly adjudicated FILD/FILA-negative AUC 0.4847, and FILD/FILA-positive AUC 0.4817. Carrier prevalence is nevertheless 39.3\% in FILD/FILA-positive subjects versus 19.2\% among explicit negatives. The pooled top 1\% ZeBRA tail shows 1.47-fold carrier enrichment ($p\approx0.009$), but the corresponding enrichment is absent within disease strata. This pattern is consistent with shared association to disease status rather than recovery of genotype from clinical history.

\subsection{Residual genomic information is localized in ZeBRA score space}
The absence of global incremental AUC does not imply that the genomic channel is conditionally uninformative everywhere. We therefore examined both clinically interpretable control-FPR bands and overlapping moving windows along the ZeBRA percentile axis. Here ``conditional LRT'' denotes the nested-model likelihood-ratio (deviance) statistic for adding one predictor after the other within a local subset. It is evidence for conditional predictive contribution, not an estimate of the individual-level likelihood-ratio factor $\LR_{G\mid C}$ in Theorem~\ref{thm:main}. These analyses therefore test a qualitative implication of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}(ii): residual genomic predictive evidence should be concentrated in restricted clinical-evidence regimes rather than distributed uniformly. They do not estimate the theorem's exact likelihood-ratio interval $[\lambda/M,\lambda/m]$, and the overlapping windows are descriptive localization analyses rather than independent confirmatory tests.

The FPR-defined analysis makes the directional asymmetry explicit. In every displayed band, the likelihood-ratio statistic for adding \textit{MUC5B} after ZeBRA ($G\mid Z$) exceeds the converse statistic for adding ZeBRA after \textit{MUC5B} ($Z\mid G$). The difference is especially large in the 5--10\% band, where $G\mid Z=16.55$ versus $Z\mid G=6.84$, and in the 20--40\% band, where $G\mid Z=13.69$ versus $Z\mid G=0.064$ (Table~\ref{tab:local-info}). The nominal $G\mid Z$ p-values in these two bands are $4.74\times10^{-5}$ and $2.15\times10^{-4}$, respectively. Because the bands and local tests are evaluated in the same cohort, these values are localization evidence rather than multiplicity-adjusted confirmatory inference.

The moving-window analysis in Fig.~\ref{fig:local-information}B shows the regime transition more directly. Across much of the intermediate-to-high ZeBRA axis, residual \textit{MUC5B} information substantially exceeds residual ZeBRA information. For example, at the 70th-percentile window, $G\mid Z=10.93$ while $Z\mid G\approx0.002$; at the 90th percentile the corresponding values are 19.78 and 1.23; and at 92.5 they are 20.37 and 0.41. The ordering reverses only in the extreme clinical-risk tail: at the 95th-percentile window, $Z\mid G=58.76$ versus $G\mid Z=22.86$, and at 97.5 the values are 68.12 versus 15.61. Thus the observed data exhibit a local information crossover: genomics remains conditionally useful through a substantial boundary-adjacent region, whereas the clinical-history channel becomes dominant at the most extreme ZeBRA values. This is qualitative correspondence to the theorem's geometry, not an empirical proof of A1 or A2.

The same pattern is visible without likelihood-ratio terminology. Within the moving windows, observed FILD/FILA prevalence is consistently higher among \textit{MUC5B} T-carriers than non-carriers (Fig.~\ref{fig:local-information}C). At the 70th-percentile window, prevalence is 4.28\% in carriers versus 0.98\% in non-carriers; at 90 it is 8.27\% versus 2.05\%; at 95 it is 18.45\% versus 7.75\%; and at 97.5 it is 20.48\% versus 9.89\%. Local adjusted odds ratios provide the corresponding effect-size view (Fig.~\ref{fig:local-information}D): the \textit{MUC5B} carrier OR remains several-fold across much of the risk axis, whereas the ZeBRA OR per local SD rises sharply only in the extreme tail, reaching 2.95 at the 95th percentile and 4.07 at 97.5. These complementary representations make the central empirical claim precise: the genomic signal is neither globally redundant nor uniformly useful; it is locally informative until sufficiently strong clinical evidence becomes dominant.

\subsection{Boundary-targeted \textit{MUC5B} use: held-out gain and an important control diagnostic}
The two-threshold rescue rule is evaluated leakage-free: upper and lower ZeBRA thresholds are derived from training negatives, the MUC5B rescue is applied only to untouched outer-test subjects, and a ZeBRA-only threshold is separately selected on training data to match the rescue rule's training-set FPR before test evaluation. Across the six evaluated threshold pairs, the \emph{mean} held-out sensitivity gain is positive. For the 0.5\%/10\% pair, mean sensitivity increases by 0.0446 and mean operational FPR differs from the matched ZeBRA comparator by only $-2.5\times10^{-4}$.

The existing result set also exposes an important limitation that should not be hidden. Among the small explicitly adjudicated-negative subset, the rescue rule has higher FPR than the original stringent ZeBRA baseline; for the 0.5\%/10\% rule the mean increase is 0.0666. The current analysis does \emph{not} calculate the corresponding explicitly-adjudicated-negative FPR for the fair matched-ZeBRA comparator, so this diagnostic cannot determine whether rescue is inferior to matched ZeBRA in that subset. We therefore describe the primary result as matched \emph{operational} FPR and reserve the adjudicated-negative matched comparison for a subsequent analysis.

\subsection{Stringent operating points provide direct clinical-tail correspondence}
Notebook~32 contains a more direct empirical comparison to the clinical-tail portion of the theorem than global AUC. At requested FPR 0.5\%, mean held-out LR$+$ is approximately 57.9 for ZeBRA, 4.28 for the genomic-only model, and 39.1 for the nonlinear combined model. At requested FPR 1\%, the corresponding means are 29.8, 3.62, and 26.7. Thus the evaluated clinical-history score dominates the evaluated genomic-only model in the stringent tail. Because raw ZeBRA is not calibrated onto a likelihood-ratio scale in this cohort, these LR$+$ values characterize the induced held-out decision rules rather than directly identifying a threshold $K$ on $\LR_C$. They therefore provide the appropriate operating-point correspondence to the theorem without proving either an unbounded clinical tail or a universal genomic bound $M_G$.

The supporting hybrid ROC analysis reaches the same qualitative conclusion. The empirical hybrid AUC is 0.8095 versus 0.8142 for ZeBRA; the convex hull of the union of ZeBRA and hybrid ROC points has descriptive AUC 0.8260. The hull represents an attainable operating envelope, not a separately validated classifier.

\subsection{Calibration diagnostics delimit the present theory--data mapping}
The variable historically named \texttt{predicted\_risk} is not calibrated as an absolute FILD/FILA probability in this cohort. Across the repeated notebook-32 splits, the raw ZeBRA score has mean calibration slope approximately 0.18 and expected calibration error approximately 0.57; mean raw score is about 0.59 while operational FILD/FILA prevalence is about 0.020. We therefore treat ZeBRA empirically as an ordinal clinical-history score in the current paper. Corollary~\ref{cor:zebra}, which maps a calibrated posterior probability to a likelihood ratio, remains conditional and is not invoked numerically with the raw score.
