\caption{\textbf{Integrated FILD/FILA evidence across global, local, likelihood-ratio, and operational regimes.}
(a) Held-out discrimination shows that ZeBRA is substantially stronger globally than the selected genomic panel and that the nonlinear combined learner does not improve on ZeBRA. (b) Repeated-split SHAP attribution nevertheless shows that the combined learner uses the genomic channel: ZeBRA is the dominant feature and rs35705950 \textit{MUC5B} is the most recurrent genomic contributor. (c) Directional nested-model likelihood-ratio/deviance statistics demonstrate localized residual \textit{MUC5B} information after conditioning on ZeBRA. (d) The actual cross-fitted score-level evidence channels show clinical log-LR rising strongly with ZeBRA state while carrier and non-carrier residual \textit{MUC5B} evidence remains state dependent and on a smaller scale. (e) Local adjusted effect sizes show persistent \textit{MUC5B} contribution through much of the score axis and increasing clinical contribution in the extreme tail. (f) Boundary-targeted \textit{MUC5B} rescue produces positive sensitivity gain over a training-FPR-matched ZeBRA comparator. (g) At stringent requested FPRs, ZeBRA has substantially larger held-out LR$+$ than the genomic-only learner. (h) Across the nine prespecified LR estimators, clinical log-LR exceeds the measured residual genomic scale for most subjects in the observed upper ZeBRA tail, approaching complete dominance by the 99th percentile. (i) Supporting ROC-envelope quantities show modest geometric complementarity among observed operating points; hull/envelope areas are descriptive and are not performance estimates from a separately validated classifier.}
\label{fig:integrated-fild}
\end{figure*}%
