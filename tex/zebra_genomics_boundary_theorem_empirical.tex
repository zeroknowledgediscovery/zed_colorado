\origsection{Empirical correspondence in the v1 ILD analysis}
\label{sec:empirical}

The archived \texttt{ZEBRA\_GENDRIVERS\_CLASSIFIER/v1} analysis used three adjudicated clinical targets and a targeted genomic panel assembled before model fitting. The three targets were (i) \texttt{FILD or FILA ADJUDICATED}, (ii) \texttt{NONFIBROTIC ILD ADJUDICATED}, and (iii) \texttt{NON FIBROTIC ILA ADJUDICATED}. Under the legacy notebook-03/032 construction, each target was converted to a binary endpoint and the analysis was restricted to observations with an available ZeBRA score. The resulting target-specific cohort sizes and case counts are documented in Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:supp-targets}.

The genomic matrix was assembled by matching an expanded ILD/IPF locus list to available genotype columns and taking the union with the pre-existing biological-driver panel. The archived v1 matrix contains 171 distinct variant loci, represented during discriminative modeling by three categorical one-hot states per locus. Of these, 148 variant columns were matched from the expanded \texttt{MORE\_LOCI.csv} screen before union and de-duplication with the original driver set. The complete 171-locus panel is given in Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:snp-panel}; rs35705950 (\textit{MUC5B}) is included explicitly.

Table~\ref{tab:auc-three-targets} gives the archived fixed-split AUC comparison. Across the three phenotype contexts, ZeBRA remained the stronger single channel, while adding the genomic panel did not yield a consistent global AUC improvement. The genomic-only AUCs were materially lower than the corresponding ZeBRA AUCs for all three targets.

The boundary-localization prediction is more directly tested by repeated outer splits for the FILD/FILA target. Figure~\ref{fig:auc-boundary} shows that a fixed regional \textit{MUC5B} rule does not improve global AUC: over 100 splits, mean AUC was 0.8160 for raw ZeBRA and 0.8135 for the regional hybrid. Nevertheless, at the nominal 5\% FPR operating point the same rule increased mean held-out sensitivity from 0.4576 to 0.4969 while the realized FPR changed only from 0.0586 to 0.0591. Mean LR$+$ increased from 7.89 to 8.46 and mean LR$-$ decreased from 0.576 to 0.535 (Table~\ref{tab:boundary5}). Thus the observed effect is concentrated at an operating boundary despite negligible---indeed slightly unfavorable---global AUC change.

Figure~\ref{fig:boundary-performance} shows the corresponding matched-FPR behavior across the evaluated operating points. The largest sensitivity gain appears around the intermediate 5\% FPR boundary rather than in the most extreme clinical tail. This is the qualitative pattern predicted by Theorem~\ref{thm:main}: bounded genomic evidence can be useful where the clinical likelihood ratio is sufficiently close to the chosen threshold, while it has progressively less ability to alter decisions when clinical evidence is far from that threshold.

The fixed regional rule used \textit{MUC5B} information within ZeBRA percentile intervals 0--50, 65--75, and 85--95, and used the calibrated ZeBRA score outside those regions. These regions were selected after inspection of the local-information curves and are therefore exploratory. Across 100 repeated outer splits, the training-split median \textit{MUC5B} odds ratios were 2.88, 4.40, and 4.07 in the three respective regions (Supplementary Fig.~\ref{fig:supp-muc5b-or}).

Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:supp-boundary} reports the repeated-split operating-point results. The nominal target FPR is used only to determine a threshold in each training split; the table reports the mean realized held-out FPR. The 5\% operating point shows the clearest boundary effect: sensitivity increased by 0.0393 on average while realized FPR changed by 0.0005. The mean global AUC difference was -0.0025 at every operating point because the same pair of continuous raw/hybrid scores was evaluated before thresholding.
