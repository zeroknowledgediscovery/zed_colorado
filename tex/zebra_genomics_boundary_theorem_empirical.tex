\subsection{Cohort, endpoint geometry, and analysis sequence}

The current analysis uses the Colorado genomic--clinical cohort assembled in \texttt{zebra\_comp}. Three stored phenotype fields are evaluated: \texttt{FILD or FILA ADJUDICATED}, \texttt{NONFIBROTIC ILD ADJUDICATED}, and \texttt{NON FIBROTIC ILA ADJUDICATED}. The operational notebook target maps \texttt{Y/y} to 1 and \texttt{N/n} to 0, converts the field to numeric, assigns missing target values to 0, and excludes observations without an available 104-week ZeBRA score. The reported endpoint is therefore the notebook-defined operational prediction target; not every target-zero observation is an independently adjudicated negative. This distinction is retained explicitly when interpreting false-positive rates.

The processed genomic matrix contains 171 selected variant loci represented by categorical genotype states; the \textit{MUC5B} promoter variant rs35705950 is included explicitly. The empirical sequence is deliberately hierarchical. Notebook~32 first asks whether genomics alone or a broad nonlinear ZeBRA+genomics model improves global held-out discrimination. Notebook~33 then asks the more specific paired question of incremental genomic value conditional on ZeBRA using a regularized model evaluated on the same held-out subjects. Separate scripts then test whether ZeBRA reconstructs \textit{MUC5B}, where residual \textit{MUC5B} information lies in ZeBRA score space, whether it can rescue boundary-adjacent subjects at matched FPR, and how the two channels occupy the low-FPR ROC geometry.

\subsection{Global discrimination: clinical history dominates}

The principal global comparison uses 30 repeated stratified outer splits with 40\% training and 60\% held-out evaluation. Within each split, ZeBRA is evaluated as the existing clinical-history score, a genomic-only LightGBM model is fitted to the genomic variables, and a combined LightGBM model is fitted to genomics plus ZeBRA. Hyperparameter selection occurs inside the training portion of each split.

Across the repeated held-out splits, ZeBRA is the strongest predictor for all three endpoints. For FILD/FILA, mean AUC is 0.8142 for ZeBRA, 0.5662 for genomics alone, and 0.7988 for the nonlinear combined model. The same qualitative ordering is stronger for the two nonfibrotic outcomes (Table~\ref{tab:auc-repeated}). Figure~\ref{fig:global-repeated-boxplots} shows the complete split-level distributions generated by notebook~32 rather than only their means. These results supersede the earlier single fixed-split comparison as the primary global-discrimination analysis.

The overlap of marginal boxplots is not a significance test because the same repeated splits are shared by the models. More importantly, the scientific question is not whether one particular combined LightGBM learner is significantly worse than ZeBRA; it is whether genomic information yields a reproducible \emph{incremental gain} once ZeBRA is already known. Notebook~33 was designed around that paired question.

\subsection{Paired incremental-value analysis: essentially zero global FILD/FILA gain}

Notebook~33 compares a ZeBRA-only logistic model with a regularized elastic-net model containing ZeBRA plus the complete genomic panel, using the same held-out subjects within each of 30 repeated splits. For FILD/FILA, mean AUC is 0.81250 for ZeBRA and 0.81293 for ZeBRA plus genomics. The paired mean difference is only $+0.00043$, the median difference is exactly 0, and the split-wise 2.5th--97.5th percentile interval is $[-0.00507,0.00408]$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:incremental-auc}). Thus the data provide no evidence of a meaningful global FILD/FILA AUC increase from broad genomic augmentation.

The proper scoring rules are at least as important as AUC. For FILD/FILA, adding all genomic variables decreases average precision by 0.0311 on average and worsens Brier score and log loss in all 30 splits. For the two nonfibrotic endpoints, global genomic augmentation also decreases mean AUC (Table~\ref{tab:incremental-metrics}). This cross-model agreement is important: the absence of global genomic gain is not merely a peculiarity of the nonlinear learner in notebook~32.

The central empirical conclusion is therefore deliberately narrow: the selected genomic panel is biologically relevant, but broad population-wide augmentation supplies essentially no robust global predictive gain conditional on this near-term longitudinal clinical-history score.

\subsection{The null global increment is not explained by genotype reconstruction}

One possible explanation for weak genomic increment would be that the longitudinal clinical record has implicitly reconstructed the strongest genomic signal. We tested that possibility directly. Among 12,766 subjects with a called rs35705950 genotype, 2,499 were \textit{MUC5B} T-allele carriers (19.6\%). ZeBRA predicts carrier status at essentially chance level: raw AUC 0.5088, five-fold cross-validated linear-logistic AUC 0.5040, spline-logistic AUC 0.5021, Pearson $r=0.0107$ ($p=0.228$), and Spearman $\rho=0.0120$ ($p=0.174$).

There is nevertheless an instructive pooled tail effect. The top 1\% of the ZeBRA distribution shows 1.47-fold \textit{MUC5B} carrier enrichment, with permutation-adjusted $p\approx0.009$. If considered alone, this can create the impression that ZeBRA has learned the genotype. The disease-stratified analysis shows otherwise. Among explicitly adjudicated FILD/FILA-negative subjects, continuous ZeBRA-to-carrier AUC is 0.485 and point-biserial $r=-0.032$ ($p=0.541$); among FILD/FILA-positive subjects, AUC is 0.482 and $r=0.013$ ($p=0.841$). In contrast, carrier prevalence is 39.3\% among FILD/FILA-positive subjects and 19.2\% among explicitly adjudicated negatives. Figure~\ref{fig:muc5b-apparent-stratified} shows this pooled-to-stratified progression directly.

\figMUCfiveBApparentAndStratified

The interpretation is important for the information-channel argument. Clinical history and germline susceptibility remain statistically distinct channels. Their pooled association can emerge because both are associated with disease, even when the clinical score contains essentially no information about genotype conditional on disease status. The relevant predictive quantity is therefore the residual disease evidence supplied by $G$ at a particular clinical state, represented theoretically by $\LR_{G\mid C}$, not the marginal ability of ZeBRA to predict $G$.

\subsection{Residual genomic information is localized in ZeBRA score space}

The absence of global incremental AUC does not imply zero conditional genomic information everywhere. We evaluated FILD/FILA prediction in regions of ZeBRA score space using both sliding/local analyses and bands defined by control-FPR thresholds. Figure~\ref{fig:local-information} preserves the richer plots generated directly by \texttt{test\_local\_zebra\_genomics\_predictive\_curves.py}. The residual contribution of \textit{MUC5B} changes markedly across the score distribution rather than remaining spatially uniform.

The FPR-defined bands provide an especially transparent numerical summary (Table~\ref{tab:local-info}). In the 5--10\% FPR band, the likelihood-ratio statistic for adding \textit{MUC5B} after ZeBRA is 16.55 ($p=4.74\times10^{-5}$), while local AUC is 0.677 for \textit{MUC5B} versus 0.367 for ZeBRA. Significant conditional genomic information is also observed in the 2--5\%, 10--20\%, and 20--40\% bands, with different effect magnitudes. The result is not that genomics becomes globally superior, but that its residual information is concentrated in restricted clinical-score regimes.

These analyses provide the most direct empirical bridge to Theorem~\ref{thm:main}(ii). The theorem does not say that genomics ceases to alter posterior probabilities away from the boundary; it says that under bounded residual genomic evidence, genomics can change a binary likelihood-ratio decision only within a bounded clinical-evidence neighborhood of the operating threshold. The observed heterogeneity is qualitatively consistent with that geometry.

\subsection{Boundary-targeted \textit{MUC5B} use improves the operating decision}

We therefore tested a clinically interpretable two-threshold rule rather than another population-wide combined classifier. Patients above a stringent upper ZeBRA threshold are classified directly by ZeBRA. Patients below a lower threshold remain negative. Only patients between the two thresholds are eligible for \textit{MUC5B}-based rescue. Both thresholds are estimated from the training split, the rescue rule is evaluated on held-out subjects, and a ZeBRA-only threshold is then selected to match the realized FPR of the rescue rule.

Across 100 repeated splits, this boundary rule increases sensitivity at essentially matched overall FPR across all evaluated threshold pairs. With an upper threshold corresponding to 0.5\% training-control FPR and a lower threshold corresponding to 10\%, mean sensitivity increases from 0.3587 for matched-FPR ZeBRA to 0.4033 with \textit{MUC5B} rescue ($\Delta=+0.0446$), while mean FPR is 0.02335 versus 0.02310. Mean LR$+$ increases from 15.58 to 17.54 and mean LR$-$ decreases from 0.657 to 0.611. The paired sensitivity advantage is positive in 97\% of the 100 splits. A narrower 0.5--5\% band yields a 0.0366 mean sensitivity gain at essentially identical FPR.

Figure~\ref{fig:boundary-rescue} uses the original outputs from \texttt{test\_muc5b\_zebra\_rescue\_matched\_fpr\_lr.py} because they show the operating effect more clearly than a reduced bar chart: sensitivity rises, the FPR change remains approximately zero, and LR$+$ improves. Table~\ref{tab:boundary-selected} gives selected numerical operating points.

This is the central empirical result for the paper. A genome-wide or candidate-panel signal can be globally unhelpful when appended indiscriminately to a strong clinical predictor, while a specific genomic factor remains actionable for subjects near an operating boundary. The relevant endpoint is therefore not necessarily a higher population-wide AUC; it can be a better sensitivity--specificity tradeoff at a fixed clinical operating regime.

\subsection{Low-FPR ROC geometry and clinical-tail dominance}

Theorem~\ref{thm:main}(iii)--(v) concerns increasingly stringent operating points. A separate five-fold out-of-fold analysis compared ZeBRA with an exploratory fixed-region \textit{MUC5B} hybrid and the convex geometry of the union of their ROC points. Empirical/interpolated AUC is 0.8142 for ZeBRA and 0.8095 for the hybrid, while the upper convex hull of the union has AUC 0.8260. The hull is not a third scalar classifier and is not reported as a deployable performance estimate. It is an operating envelope demonstrating that the channels occupy different favorable ROC regions.

Figure~\ref{fig:low-fpr-geometry} focuses on the low-FPR portion of this analysis. Together with the high LR$+$ values obtained by stringent ZeBRA thresholds in the matched-FPR experiments, the plot is qualitatively consistent with the theorem's claim that sufficiently extreme clinical evidence can dominate a bounded static channel. The Colorado data do not establish A1 or an asymptotic limit; the figure is presented as empirical correspondence rather than proof.

\figLowFPRGeometry

\subsection{Empirical synthesis}

Taken together, the analyses support a more precise claim than either ``genomics does not help'' or ``multimodal combination always helps.'' First, the current near-term clinical-history score is much stronger globally than the selected genomic panel. Second, two very different global combination strategies---nonlinear LightGBM and paired regularized logistic modeling---provide no reproducible FILD/FILA discrimination gain after ZeBRA is known. Third, this is not because ZeBRA reconstructs the strongest common fibrotic-risk genotype. Fourth, residual \textit{MUC5B} disease information is heterogeneous in ZeBRA score space. Fifth, using that genomic information selectively near a decision boundary improves held-out sensitivity and LR$+$ at essentially unchanged FPR.

This sequence is the empirical analogue of regime-dependent complementarity. Genomic susceptibility remains biologically important and statistically distinct, but its incremental \emph{decision} value contracts toward regions where the clinical evidence remains close enough to the chosen threshold for bounded residual genomic evidence to change the action.

\subsection{Limitations of the empirical correspondence}

Several qualifications are important. The genomic panel is selected rather than a complete externally weighted IPF polygenic risk score. The local regions and threshold pairs are evaluated within the same Colorado resource and require prospective prespecification or independent replication. Repeated train/test splits share subjects and summarize robustness to partitioning; they are not independent biological replicates and their split-wise quantiles are not classical independent-sample confidence intervals. The low-FPR convex hull is an operating envelope, not a deployable third classifier. Most importantly, the operational notebook target assigns missing adjudication fields to zero. Overall false-positive rates therefore refer to that operational endpoint and should not be equated with FPR among fully adjudicated controls without a separate ascertainment analysis.
