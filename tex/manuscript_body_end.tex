\section{Scope and limitations}

The theory is a statement about information channels, not about a particular machine-learning architecture. It does not require independence between genomics and clinical history; dependence is retained explicitly through $\LR_{G\mid C}$. The marginal genomic power result also propagates through deterministic or class-independent randomized post-processing of the measured genomic state, as formalized in Corollary~\ref{cor:genomic-postprocessing}.

Several qualifications are essential. First, the direct empirical likelihood-ratio analysis estimates a finite-stage control-side residual-genomic scale for the measured \textit{MUC5B} channel. It does not establish the optional uniform-tightness condition A1-U across clinical-history lengths, does not establish the same bound under the case law, and does not imply a universal genome-wide bound. Second, the deterministic bounded-residual corollary remains a useful idealization but is a stronger special case. Third, A2 is an explicit assumption about the existence of arbitrarily strong clinical-evidence tails; sequential factorization makes accumulation possible but does not prove unboundedness, and the Colorado data demonstrate only a strong observed tail over a finite range. Fourth, Theorem~\ref{thm:main}(ii) concerns threshold reversals, not all posterior-risk changes. Fifth, the current genomic comparator is a selected panel and the direct residual-LR analysis focuses on \textit{MUC5B}; neither should be interpreted as an empirical bound on genome-wide predictors, external polygenic risk scores, or unmeasured genomic information. Sixth, the raw ZeBRA score is not calibrated as an absolute posterior probability in this cohort, so Corollary~\ref{cor:zebra} is not numerically applied; the empirical connection instead uses direct score-level LR estimation. Seventh, the overlapping local windows and FPR bands are descriptive localization analyses, not confirmatory independent tests; nominal local p-values are not used as primary evidence. Eighth, the operational FILD/FILA endpoint includes missing adjudication values in the target-zero comparison group. The rescue rule is therefore supported at matched operational FPR, but its benefit within explicitly adjudicated negatives remains unresolved until a matched-ZeBRA comparator is computed in that subset. Finally, the nonfibrotic endpoints contain only 74 and 27 positives and are treated solely as sparse ancillary checks, while all rescue and LR analyses remain internal and require prospective or external validation.

A stronger future validation should therefore calibrate ZeBRA explicitly onto a likelihood-ratio or posterior-probability scale, estimate uncertainty for the direct LR quantities, evaluate prespecified fixed-specificity thresholds prospectively, compute the matched-ZeBRA comparator within explicitly adjudicated negatives, and replicate boundary-localized genomic rescue in an external cohort.

\section{Conclusion}

The central result is not that clinical history ``contains'' genomics, nor that genomics is globally useless. The two modalities are distinct evidence channels. Bayes' rule factorizes their joint evidence into a clinical likelihood ratio and a residual genomic likelihood ratio conditional on clinical state. The key theoretical refinement is that finite-stage boundary localization is stated directly in terms of the high-probability residual-genomic scale at the clinical stage being analyzed; it does not require a bound uniform over all history lengths. Uniform tightness is introduced only as an optional stronger assumption for sequential clinical-tail dominance. Under the deterministic special case, the exact boundary band is recovered; under a marginal genomic likelihood-ratio bound, sufficiently extreme clinical evidence dominates any genomic-only rule whenever the corresponding clinical-tail condition holds.

The current Colorado results show direct empirical correspondence with this geometry. ZeBRA dominates global discrimination; broad genomic augmentation provides essentially zero incremental FILD/FILA AUC after conditioning on ZeBRA; ZeBRA does not reconstruct \textit{MUC5B}; residual \textit{MUC5B} disease information is heterogeneous across selected ZeBRA operating bands; a boundary-targeted rescue rule improves mean sensitivity and LR$+$ at closely matched operational FPR; and the direct LR analysis estimates stable 95\% and 99\% control-side residual-genomic scales, demonstrates that clinical log-LR clearly exceeds residual genomic scale in the extreme observed ZeBRA tail, and shows that genomic-induced decision flips remain close to the clinical boundary. The resulting picture is one of \emph{regime-dependent complementarity}: genomics can matter locally and operationally even when its global incremental discrimination is minimal.

Because the direct LR analysis remains internal to the current cohort and focuses on the measured MUC5B residual channel, it should be understood as empirical support for finite-stage boundary geometry rather than as proof of A1-U, A2, or universal genomic boundedness. Even so, the revised theory and data now align more tightly: the assumptions are explicitly separated by strength, and the central claims are supported by figures and tables that display the relevant likelihood-ratio quantities without overextending the empirical scope.
