\section{Scope and limitations}

The theory is a statement about information channels, not about a particular machine-learning architecture. It does not require independence between genomics and clinical history; dependence is retained explicitly through $\LR_{G\mid C}$. The central theorem is finite-stage: Theorem~\ref{thm:main}(ii) requires only a finite residual-genomic quantile at the clinical stage being analyzed. The stronger assumptions A1-U and A2 enter only the conditional sequential and extreme-tail extensions.

Several qualifications are essential. First, the direct empirical likelihood-ratio analysis estimates a finite-stage control-side residual-genomic scale for the measured \textit{MUC5B} channel. It does not establish A1-U across clinical-history lengths, does not establish the same bound under the case law, and does not imply a universal genome-wide bound. Second, the deterministic bounded-residual corollary remains a stronger special case. Third, A2 explicitly assumes that arbitrarily strong positive clinical evidence is attainable on rare trajectories. Sequential factorization makes evidence accumulation possible but does not imply mathematical unboundedness; the Colorado data demonstrate only a strong observed tail over a finite range. Fourth, Theorem~\ref{thm:main}(ii) concerns threshold reversals, not all posterior-risk changes. Fifth, the current genomic comparator is a selected panel and the direct residual-LR analysis focuses on \textit{MUC5B}; neither should be interpreted as an empirical bound on genome-wide predictors, external polygenic risk scores, or unmeasured genomic information. Sixth, the raw ZeBRA score is not calibrated as an absolute posterior probability in this cohort, so Corollary~\ref{cor:zebra} is not numerically applied; the empirical connection instead uses direct score-level LR estimation. Seventh, the overlapping local windows and FPR bands are descriptive localization analyses. Their nested-model LR/deviance statistics are evidence of conditional predictive contribution, not estimates of the individual-level residual likelihood ratio in the theorem. Eighth, the operational FILD/FILA endpoint includes missing adjudication values in the target-zero comparison group. The adjudicated-negative rescue comparison is therefore retained as a control diagnostic, but its small per-split sample size precludes a definitive superiority claim. Finally, the nonfibrotic endpoints contain only 74 and 27 positives and are treated solely as sparse ancillary checks, while all rescue and LR analyses remain internal and require prospective or external validation.

A stronger future validation should calibrate ZeBRA explicitly onto a likelihood-ratio or posterior-probability scale, estimate uncertainty for the direct LR quantities, evaluate prespecified fixed-specificity thresholds prospectively, enlarge or externally replicate the explicitly adjudicated-negative comparison, and replicate boundary-localized genomic utility in an external cohort.

\section{Conclusion}

The central result is not that clinical history ``contains'' genomics, nor that genomics is globally useless. The two modalities are distinct evidence channels. Bayes' rule factorizes their joint evidence into a clinical likelihood ratio and a residual genomic likelihood ratio conditional on clinical state. The main theorem shows that, at a fixed clinical stage, the high-probability scale of residual genomic evidence determines a neighborhood of the clinical decision boundary outside which genomic-induced decision reversals are rare. This finite-stage result requires neither uniformity over clinical-history length nor an unbounded clinical-evidence tail.

The Colorado analysis directly instantiates that finite-stage geometry at the observed ZeBRA-score level. Broad genomic augmentation produces essentially zero paired incremental FILD/FILA AUC after conditioning on ZeBRA; ZeBRA does not reconstruct \textit{MUC5B}; residual \textit{MUC5B} predictive contribution varies strongly across the ZeBRA axis; and the cross-fitted direct LR analysis estimates stable 95\% and 99\% control-side residual-genomic scales while showing that genomic-induced decision flips remain close to the clinical boundary. The two-threshold rescue analysis is consistent with the practical consequence of this geometry, but is secondary to the direct LR evidence.

The observed extreme ZeBRA tail also shows clinical log-LR dominating the measured residual genomic scale, and stringent held-out operating points favor ZeBRA over the genomic-only model. These findings are finite-range correspondence to the conditional tail-extension theory, not proof of A1-U, A2, or universal genomic boundedness. The resulting picture is one of \emph{regime-dependent complementarity}: genomics can matter locally and operationally even when its global incremental discrimination is minimal.
