\section{Why the clinical channel can have an extended tail}

The theorem does not assume that every later clinical event is informative, nor that disease becomes perfectly observable. It requires only that the clinical channel can accumulate sufficiently strong evidence on some disease trajectories. For a sequential history $C_n=(X_1,\ldots,X_n)$, the clinical likelihood ratio factorizes exactly as
\begin{equation}
\LR_C(C_n)
=\prod_{k=1}^{n}
\frac{p(X_k\mid Y=1,X_{<k})}
     {p(X_k\mid Y=0,X_{<k})},
\label{eq:sequentialLR}
\end{equation}
so that
\begin{equation}
\log\LR_C(C_n)
=\sum_{k=1}^{n}
\log
\frac{p(X_k\mid Y=1,X_{<k})}
     {p(X_k\mid Y=0,X_{<k})}.
\label{eq:sequentiallogLR}
\end{equation}
This is the standard likelihood-ratio accumulation underlying sequential testing and information accumulation \cite{Wald1945,CoverThomas2006}. The genomic state does not acquire new germline variants as disease approaches, whereas the clinical history can acquire additional diagnoses, procedures, prescriptions, and comorbidities. The difference is therefore not that clinical and genomic predictors obey different probability laws; both enter Bayes' rule as evidence. At any fixed clinical stage, Theorem~\ref{thm:main}(ii) measures the residual genomic scale by a control-side quantile $b_{\delta,n}$. The stronger A1-U condition is needed only to keep that residual scale uniformly tight as the clinical record lengthens; under Corollary~\ref{cor:deterministic-band} the same idea sharpens to a literal deterministic bound.

Equation \eqref{eq:sequentialLR} also clarifies the sense in which clinical odds may rise sharply: likelihood evidence adds in log space but multiplies in odds space. No claim of exponential growth follows from AUC alone. Nor does the sequential factorization prove A2. It shows only how evidence can accumulate; A2 separately assumes that arbitrarily strong positive clinical-evidence tails are attainable on some trajectories.

\section{Interpretation of the theoretical result}

The revised theory makes five distinct points that should not be conflated.

\subsection{Global discrimination is not the quantity controlled by the theorem}
Theorem~\ref{thm:main}(ii) controls where genomics can change a threshold decision, not the global ranking of all case--control pairs. If genomic information matters only in a narrow decision band, then only a restricted fraction of pairs can be reordered. Proposition~\ref{prop:auc-local} makes the consequence explicit: meaningful local operating-point improvement can coexist with negligible global AUC change.

\subsection{Weak ZeBRA--genotype association does not imply zero conditional genomic disease information}
The relevant residual quantity is $\LR_{G\mid C}$, not the marginal dependence between ZeBRA and genotype. ZeBRA can be nearly uninformative about rs35705950 carrier status while \textit{MUC5B} still changes disease odds conditional on a given clinical state.

\subsection{Finite-stage localization does not require uniformity over history length}
At a fixed observed clinical stage, Theorem~\ref{thm:main}(ii) is expressed through the finite-stage residual-evidence quantile $b_{\delta,n}$ and requires no common bound over all $n$. A1-U is an optional stronger assumption used only for sequential tail dominance. This distinction is important biologically and empirically: the Colorado analysis estimates a score-level residual-genomic scale for \textit{MUC5B}, but does not establish uniform tightness for arbitrary genomic features as clinical history accumulates. Corollary~\ref{cor:deterministic-band} recovers the exact boundary band as a stronger special case.

\subsection{Operating-threshold dependence is a feature, not a defect}
Corollary~\ref{cor:zebra} shows, for a calibrated posterior score, that the decision-relevant genomic region is defined relative to the chosen threshold. There is no threshold-free universal ``useful band.'' Changing the tolerated false-positive rate moves the clinical boundary and therefore changes which patients are potentially reclassified by genomics. Because the raw Colorado ZeBRA score is not calibrated as a posterior probability, this corollary is not applied numerically in the empirical section.

\subsection{Clinical-tail dominance is a stringent-operating-point statement}
Proposition~\ref{prop:genomicpower} and Theorem~\ref{thm:main}(iii) concern the upper disease-evidence likelihood-ratio tail, explicitly $\{\log\Lambda_{C_n}\ge K>0\}$. The empirically relevant quantities are therefore direct likelihood-ratio estimates, decision-flip localization, and stringent-specificity LR$+$ behavior rather than global AUC alone. The data can support the observed tail geometry over the measured range; they do not prove the mathematical unboundedness assumption A2.
